The mechanic, in full

One fix.
One cell. One owner.

A claim isn't a tap on a map. It's your real position, resolved to a hexagon on our servers, checked, and written as ownership. Scroll to watch a single cell change hands.

01 · Location

You are somewhere real.

The app reads your precise GPS position while the map is open. Nothing about the world moves until you do.

lat 39.7411 · lng 64.1108 · Bukhara
02 · The cell

Your point falls in one hexagon.

The server converts your coordinates to a single H3 cell — about a football pitch across. You never name the cell; it is derived from where you stand.

h3 · res 9 · 8928308280fffff
03 · Validation

The fix has to be worth trusting.

A claim needs accuracy within 30 metres, and the movement has to be plausible. Spoofed or teleporting fixes are refused before anything is written.

accuracy 8 m · mock false · accepted
04 · Ownership

The server writes the ground to you.

Only the server decides ownership — your device can't assert it. The cell is stamped with your identity and fills with your colour.

owner ← you · captured_at now
05 · Territory

Cells become a shape.

Hold neighbouring ground and it knits into territory — the same geometry, seen the same way by everyone on the map, in real time.

realtime · authoritative geometry
06 · Persistence

It holds until it's taken.

Close the app, come back tomorrow — your ground is still yours, restored from the server. It changes only when someone walks in and claims it.

survives restart · server-restored

Why it's built this way

Presence is the only currency.

No purchase, no tap, no shortcut buys a cell. Because ownership is decided server-side from a validated fix, the only way onto the board is to actually be there — which is what makes holding ground mean something.
